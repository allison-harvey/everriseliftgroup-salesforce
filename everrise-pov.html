Before anything else — this is not a proposal to replace FIELDBOSS

FIELDBOSS is built inside Dynamics, not bolted on. Work orders, safety tests, equipment histories, violations and job costing sit on the same records as your Business Central financials. That's a decade of elevator-specific logic, and re-implementing it would cost you a year to end up where you started.

It stays your system of record. Everything below sits above it or beside it. If you ever hear otherwise from my side, I'd want to know.

What we'd actually be

The four pieces that would matter to a consolidating roll-up.

Four separate products. You wouldn't need all four, and two you may already pay for without thinking of them as ours.

Data Cloud the data layer not a database you migrate into

Queries data where it already lives — Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift — without copying it. Your lake stays your lake, in your account, in its own format.

At EverRise Your lake doesn't move. What changes is that the things asking it questions get an identity, a permission model and a record of what they asked.
MuleSoft the integration layer APIs between systems that were never meant to talk

Puts an API in front of systems that don't have one and keeps that contract stable when the system behind it changes. It has no opinion about whether you use our CRM.

At EverRise Something has to broker between Dynamics, Business Central, the lake and whatever Gen-L and All City arrived with. That's this product's whole job.
Agentforce the action layer agents that do things, not a chatbot

An agent gets a defined job, the permissions to do it, and an audit trail. A chatbot answers; an agent acts and leaves a record. Claude runs as the reasoning model inside it.

At EverRise "Which units are past their inspection window" is a question Claude already answers. "Assigned, escalated as it ages, and written back to the equipment record" is this layer.
Slack where it surfaces yes, Slack is us

An agent nobody sees is an agent nobody uses. The overdue-inspection alert has to land where the compliance manager already is, not in a portal they have to remember.

At EverRise If you're a Teams shop that's fine — but worth me knowing, because it changes what the first useful thing looks like.

And what you almost certainly don't need from us

I'd rather rule these out in writing than have you find out later I was quietly hoping to sell them:

  • Sales Cloud — the pipeline product. You're a service business on recurring contracts. Unless you're forecasting a modernisation sales motion across the group, this isn't your problem.
  • Our field service product. We have one. FIELDBOSS is built for elevator contractors and ours isn't. Yours is the better tool.
  • Marketing Cloud. Nothing about a consolidating elevator roll-up suggests you need campaign automation right now.

Which leaves the data, integration and action layers — the three with anything to do with what you told me you're building.

The question I asked badly on the call

There's a real difference between asking your data a question and having something act on the answer.

Querying a lake with Claude is useful and puts you ahead of your peers. What it doesn't give you is an owner, a permission model, an audit trail, or a route back into the system of record. That's what I was reaching for and didn't say.

For whoever Chris forwards this to

Five questions I can't answer from the outside — and two of them could rule this out.

I'd rather send questions than a deck. The answers decide whether there's a conversation at all.

What is the lake actually built on?

Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift, Microsoft Fabric, or object storage with a catalog over it?

Why it decides things: Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and Redshift we read in place. Fabric we don't, yet — and if you built on Fabric, which would be reasonable given Dynamics, none of the read-in-place story applies to you today.

Is FIELDBOSS data in the lake, or only the financials?

Work orders, safety tests, equipment histories and violations — or just what comes out of Business Central?

Why it decides things: If the operational data never reaches the lake, Claude is reasoning over financial outcomes rather than field reality.

One lake, or one per brand?

Did Century, Avery, Gen-L, ETI and All City get consolidated into a single lake, or is each still landing separately?

Why it decides things: This is the difference between a reporting project and an integration project. If it's still five, the sequencing matters more than the tooling, and that's a conversation worth having on its own.

How are you calling Claude today?

Direct API, through Bedrock, the desktop app, or something your team built?

Why it decides things: If you're already on Bedrock you're most of the way to running inside a governed boundary and this is a smaller change than it sounds. If it's the desktop app, what you have today is one or two people doing something clever rather than something the business can depend on — still valuable, different starting point.

Who owns identity and permissions for lake access?

Is there a model for who can see which brand's data, or is lake access currently all-or-nothing?

Why it decides things: This is the one that quietly kills these projects. Every governed-agent design assumes an answer; mid-consolidation there usually isn't one yet.

One thing worth knowing, since you're already on Claude

Since 26 August, Claude is the first model running fully inside the Salesforce trust boundary, through Bedrock, and as a reasoning model in Agentforce.

So the work your team has already done isn't something you'd unwind. Same model. The only question is whether you want a permission and action layer around it or would rather build that yourself — and building it yourself is a real option I'd want to understand before arguing against.
