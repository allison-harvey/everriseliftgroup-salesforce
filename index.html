Before anything else

This is not a proposal to replace FIELDBOSS.

FIELDBOSS stays the system of record for elevator service. Everything in this document sits above it or beside it.

FIELDBOSS is built inside Dynamics 365 rather than bolted onto it. Work orders, safety tests, equipment histories, violations and job costing live on the same records as your Business Central financials, with elevator-specific logic on top of that. It is a decade of vertical engineering built for exactly your trade, and re-implementing it would be a bad idea that cost you a year and bought you nothing.

You told me you're heavily embedded in it. That's the right place to be, and if anyone from my company suggests otherwise, they haven't read your business.

FIELDBOSS architecture per its own published product documentation. Everything below concerns the layer above the systems of record, not the systems themselves.

What I actually meant

There's a real difference between asking your data a question and having something act on the answer.

Querying a lake with Claude is genuinely useful and puts you ahead of most of your peers. What it doesn't give you is anything that carries the answer forward. Three stages — you're solidly at the first.

✓
You already have this

Retrieve — ask the lake a question

Claude reads the lake and answers. Fast, flexible, needs nobody's approval to try. This is the right place to have started and I wouldn't change it.

2
The part most people skip

Reason — with governance attached

The same question, except the model only sees what the person asking is allowed to see, and every answer traces back to the records behind it.

Since August, Claude runs fully inside the Salesforce trust boundary, delivered through Amazon Bedrock — the first model provider to do so. Not a different model from the one your team is using. The same one, with an identity and permission layer around it.

3
This is the gap

Act — and write it back

An agent that takes the answer and does something with it: assigns the overdue inspection, escalates it as it ages, notifies the person who owns that jurisdiction, and writes the outcome back to the equipment record.

The write-back is the point. If the action doesn't land in FIELDBOSS, your system of record is now wrong in a new way — which is worse than having no agent at all.

Put plainly: "which units across seven brands are past their inspection window" is a question Claude can already answer for you today. "That list is assigned to the right person, escalated when it ages, and written back where an inspector can see it" is the part that doesn't exist yet. Whether that gap is worth closing now is entirely your call — and it may well not be, four days after closing All City.

What we'd actually be

Four pieces, none of them a pipeline.

Each is a separate product. You would not need all four, and one of them you may already be paying for without thinking of it as ours.

Data Cloud · the data layer

Not a database you migrate into

It queries data where it already lives — Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift — without copying or migrating it. Your lake stays your lake, in your account, in its own format, read through an open table format rather than imported.

At EverRiseThe lake you're building stays exactly where it is. What changes is that the things asking it questions gain an identity, a permission model, and a record of what they asked. This is the layer that turns "we query the lake" into something the business can rely on rather than something two clever people do.
MuleSoft · the integration layer

APIs between systems that were never meant to talk

It puts a stable API in front of systems that don't have one, and holds that contract steady when the system behind it changes. Deliberately agnostic — it brokers Microsoft estates all day and has no opinion about whether you use our CRM.

At EverRiseSeven companies, five of them arriving since 2024, each with its own stack. Whatever consolidation you're doing, something has to broker between Dynamics, Business Central, the lake, and whatever All City brought with it. That is this product's entire job.
Agentforce · the action layer

Agents that do things, not a chatbot

An agent gets a defined job, the permissions to do it, a limited set of tools it may call, and an audit trail of what it did. The distinction that matters: a chatbot answers, an agent acts and leaves a record. Claude runs as a reasoning model inside it.

At EverRiseThis is stage three from the section above. It's the only place where the inspection example becomes real rather than illustrative — and it's the piece I'd want your colleague to pressure-test hardest, because it depends entirely on what the lake actually contains.
Slack · where it surfaces

Yes, Slack is us

We bought Slack in 2021. It matters here because an agent nobody sees is an agent nobody uses — an alert has to arrive where someone already is, not in a portal they have to remember to open. Claude runs directly in Slack too.

At EverRiseIf your brands are already on Slack, you're already a customer of ours and the surface for all of this exists today. If you're a Teams shop, that's fine and not a blocker — it just changes what the first useful thing looks like. Worth me knowing which.

And the longer list of what you almost certainly don't need from us.

I'd rather put this in writing than have someone discover later that I was quietly hoping to sell it. This list is deliberately longer than the one above.

When people say "Salesforce" this is what they picture: contacts, accounts, opportunity stages, a seat for every salesperson. You're a service and maintenance business with recurring contracts. It's priced per seat and it earns its keep in businesses whose primary motion is new-logo selling. That isn't you, and I'm not leading with it.

✕

Our field service product

We have one. FIELDBOSS is purpose-built for elevator contractors and ours is not. Yours is the better tool for your trade, and that isn't a close call.

✕

Replacing your accounting or job costing

Business Central does that and it sits on the same records as your service data. Moving it during a seven-brand integration would be an unforced error.

✕

Marketing Cloud

Nothing about a commercial elevator platform with named-account relationships suggests you need campaign automation right now.

✕

A migration of any kind

Nothing in this document requires you to move data, replatform a brand, or decommission a system. If a proposal ever does, that's the moment to be suspicious of it.

Which leaves the data layer, the integration layer and the action layer — the three that have anything to do with what you described. Three products out of a catalogue of dozens is, I hope, a more useful answer than a platform pitch.

For your colleague

Five questions, and why each one changes the answer.

What is the lake actually built on?
Why it decides things

Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift, Microsoft Fabric, or object storage with a catalog over it?

Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and Redshift are the established read-in-place paths — we query them without copying. Microsoft Fabric is a joint Salesforce–Microsoft workstream rather than a settled capability, and given FIELDBOSS sits inside Dynamics, Fabric would be a perfectly reasonable choice for you to have made. If that's where you've built, I'd want to confirm exactly where that integration stands today rather than tell you it works — and if it doesn't yet, I'd rather say so now.

Is FIELDBOSS operational data in the lake, or only the financials?
Why it decides things

Work orders, safety tests, equipment histories and violations — or just what comes out of Business Central?

If the operational elevator data never reaches the lake, Claude is reasoning over financial outcomes rather than field reality, and the interesting questions are unanswerable no matter what sits on top. This is the single most important answer on this list.

One lake, or one per brand?
Why it decides things

Did Century, ETI, Gen-L, Berlin, Avery and All City get consolidated into a single lake, or is each still landing separately?

This is the difference between a reporting project and an integration project. If it's still several, the sequencing matters far more than the tooling — and that's a conversation worth having entirely on its own, with no product in it.

How are you calling Claude today?
Why it decides things

Direct API, through Bedrock, the desktop app, or something your team built?

If you're already on Bedrock you're most of the way to running inside a governed boundary, and this is a much smaller change than it sounds. If it's the desktop app, what you have today is one or two people doing something clever rather than something the business can depend on — still valuable, different starting point.

Who owns identity and permissions for lake access?
Why it decides things

Is there a model for who can see which brand's data, or is lake access currently all-or-nothing?

This is the one that quietly kills these projects. Every governed-agent design assumes an answer, and two years into a multi-brand integration there often isn't one yet. If there isn't, that's the actual first project, and it has nothing to buy attached to it.

Deliberately absent from this page: pricing, projected savings, and any number about your volumes. Some of what's described here is licensed software and some of it is work, and I'm not putting figures in a document that gets forwarded. That's a conversation once we know whether any of this is even relevant.
