The smallest possible illustration of the problem

Your About page says EverRise "brings together seven established elevator companies" in one paragraph, and "brings together six established elevator companies" further down the same page. Both lists are there; they don't match.

I'm not raising that to score a point — it's a website, and websites lag. I'm raising it because if the brand count is hard to hold consistent on a page that two people maintain, the harder version of that question is sitting behind it: across seven companies acquired over fourteen years, who reconciles the numbers that actually matter, and where do they live?

Before anything else

I got something wrong in my first email, and I'd rather fix it than let it sit.

In my note on 15 September I referred to FIELDBOSS as if I knew it was your service system. I didn't. I'd found a FIELDBOSS customer story about a company called Action Elevator and assumed it was you.

It wasn't. That case study is about Action Elevator Inc. of Fairfield, New Jersey — a different company that happens to share your name. Nothing published by your Action Elevator names FIELDBOSS, Dynamics, or any other system, and I should have caught that before I hit send rather than after.

I'm flagging it for a practical reason as well as an honest one. If a vendor opens by telling you which software you run and gets it wrong, the correct response is roughly the one you gave me. So: I don't know what EverRise runs for service management, and I'm not going to guess again. It's the first question in the list below, and the answer changes most of what follows.

What this does mean, regardless of the answer

Whatever runs elevator service at each brand — FIELDBOSS, Simplex, Wennsoft, TEAM, spreadsheets, or five different things — it is almost certainly the right place for work orders, safety tests, equipment history and job costing, and re-implementing it would cost you a year and buy you nothing. Vertical elevator software is a decade of trade-specific logic. I am not here to replace it, and if a colleague of mine suggests otherwise, they haven't read your business.

The one thing worth twenty minutes

You're standing up a modernization sales motion across seven brands. That's a different problem from running service.

This is the only argument in this document, and it doesn't depend on knowing your stack.

1
What's already true

Modernization has an executive, a mandate, and open headcount

A President of Modernization & Construction. H.I.G. capital earmarked for modernization capability. A Modernization Sales Representative req open right now. Three signals, three independent public sources, all pointing the same way.

2
What modernization selling requires

Knowing which units are aging, across every brand, before the customer calls

A modernization sale is triggered by equipment condition and age — controller vintage, door operator wear, entrapment history, repeat callbacks. That intelligence is generated by service, one unit at a time, and it accumulated separately inside seven companies over fourteen years.

3
The gap I'd want to test

Service data is per-brand. A modernization pipeline has to be cross-brand.

Your service systems are organised the way your companies were acquired. Your modernization P&L is organised the way the platform is going to be run. Those two shapes don't line up, and a new sales rep is about to sit in the gap between them — with a quota.

That's a reporting-and-workflow problem sitting above service, not a replacement for it. It's the only thing I'd ask you about.

One thing I'd say plainly: you spent three decades at Otis and Schindler running modernization, service growth and digital transformation, and you scaled the service organisation of a PE-backed fire and life safety company. You have seen this movie more times than I have. If your read is that the cross-brand view is a 2028 problem and route density comes first, that is a completely defensible answer and I'd rather hear it than argue with it.

Scope, honestly

What we'd plausibly be — and the larger list of what we wouldn't.

I'd rather rule things out in writing than have you discover later that I was quietly hoping to sell them. Each of these is a separate product; you would not need all of them, and the second list is deliberately longer than the first.

What I can't establish from outside

Six questions. Two of them could end this, and I'd rather they ended it now.

These are the things I previously would have guessed at. They're questions instead, because a wrong guess in writing is worth less than an honest blank.

What actually runs elevator service at each brand?
Why it decides things

One system across all seven, or one per brand, or somewhere in between. If it's already consolidated, most of what I've written above is solved and you should tell me so. If it's five systems, the sequencing conversation matters far more than any tool.

Who owns the modernization number across brands today?
Why it decides things

If Kristin already has a working cross-brand view — even a manual one someone rebuilds monthly — then the gap I described is a nuisance, not a problem, and this page overstates it.

How does a modernization rep find candidate units right now?
Why it decides things

Whether it's a report, a branch manager's memory, or a technician flagging it on site. This is the single most useful answer on the list, because it tells me whether the new rep you're hiring has a pipeline waiting for them or has to build one from scratch.

Is there a reconciled customer and unit list across the seven brands?
Why it decides things

Fourteen years of acquisitions in overlapping markets — Century, ETI, Gen-L and Berlin all in and around Greater Washington — means the same building may appear more than once. Whether that's been reconciled determines whether this is an integration project or a reporting one.

What is Doug Taylor's integration mandate, concretely?
Why it decides things

Systems, or operations, or both. If the role is operational — crews, branches, contracts — then the systems question may have no owner at all, which is the thing most worth knowing before anyone proposes anything.

Are the brands on Slack or on Teams?
Why it decides things

We own Slack, so if your brands are on it you're already a customer of ours and I should know that. If you're a Teams shop that's completely fine and not a blocker — it just changes what a first useful step would look like.

Deliberately absent from this page: any dollar figure, any projected return, any headcount estimate, and any claim about your volumes. I can't model a return without your average modernization contract value, your unit count, your modernization attach rate and your current close rate — and a number I made up would tell you nothing except that I was willing to make one up. If the conversation gets that far, we build it from your figures or not at all.
