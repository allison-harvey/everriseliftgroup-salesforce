The reframe

A sales database is one product we sell. It is almost certainly not the one you'd buy.

When people say "Salesforce" they usually picture a pipeline with opportunity stages in it. That product exists, we do sell it, and I am not going to pretend it is the answer to anything you described on our call. Here is the honest version of the gap.

What "CRM" means to most people

A place to log deals

  • Contacts, accounts, opportunity stages
  • Forecast reports for a sales leader
  • Seats for every salesperson
  • Something your sales team lives in and nobody else opens
What we'd actually be doing at EverRise

Four layers, none of them a pipeline

  • Reading your data lake without copying it anywhere
  • Connecting five acquired brands' systems by API
  • Agents that take action and write the result back
  • Doing it where your team already works, with permissions attached

To be plain about the commercial reality: some of what follows is licensed software and some of it is work. I'm not putting numbers in a document that's going to get forwarded — that's a conversation once we know whether any of this is even relevant.

Before anything else — this is not a proposal to replace FIELDBOSS

FIELDBOSS is built inside Dynamics 365, not bolted onto it. Your work orders, safety tests, equipment histories, violations and job costing sit on the same records as your Business Central financials, with a hundred-plus elevator-specific capabilities on top. That is a decade of vertical logic built for exactly your trade, and re-implementing it would be a bad idea that cost you a year.

FIELDBOSS stays the system of record for elevator service. Everything below sits above it or beside it. If anyone from my company tells you otherwise, they haven't read your business.

What we'd actually be

The four pieces that would matter to a five-brand roll-up.

Each of these is a separate product. You would not need all four, and two of them you may already be paying for without thinking of them as ours.

Data Cloud the data layer not a database you migrate into

This is the one that speaks directly to what you described. It queries data where it already lives — Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift — without copying or migrating it. Your lake stays your lake, in your account, in its own format. We read it through an open table format rather than importing it.

At EverRise The lake you're building stays exactly where it is. The difference is that the things asking it questions gain an identity, a permission model, and a record of what they asked. This is the layer that turns "we query the lake" into something the business can rely on rather than something two clever people do.
MuleSoft the integration layer APIs between systems that were never meant to talk

An integration platform we own. It puts an API in front of systems that don't have one, and keeps the contract stable when the system behind it changes. It is deliberately agnostic — it integrates Microsoft estates all day long and has no opinion about whether you use our CRM.

At EverRise Five brands acquired in twenty-four months, each arriving with its own stack, and three of them still running their own separate websites. Whatever consolidation you're doing, something has to broker between Dynamics, Business Central, the lake, and whatever Gen-L and All City brought with them. That is this product's entire job.
Agentforce the action layer agents that do things, not a chatbot

Where an AI agent is given a defined job, the permissions to do it, tools it is allowed to call, and an audit trail of what it did. The distinction that matters: a chatbot answers, an agent acts and leaves a record. Claude runs as a reasoning model inside it.

At EverRise "Which units across five brands are past their inspection window" is a question Claude can already answer for you. "That list is assigned to the right compliance manager, escalated when it ages, and written back to the equipment record where an AHJ inspector can see it" is this layer. With two compliance managers covering five brands, that gap is the whole opportunity.
Slack where it surfaces yes, Slack is us

We bought Slack in 2021. It matters here because an agent nobody sees is an agent nobody uses — the overdue-inspection alert has to arrive where the compliance manager already is, not in a portal they have to remember to open. Claude also runs directly in Slack.

At EverRise If your brands are already on Slack, you are already our customer and the surface for all of this exists today. If you're a Teams shop, that's fine and not a blocker — worth me knowing which, because it changes what the first useful thing looks like.

And what you almost certainly don't need from us

I'd rather rule things out in writing than have someone discover later that I was quietly hoping to sell them:

  • Sales Cloud — the pipeline product. You're a service and maintenance business with recurring contracts. Unless you're running a modernisation sales motion that needs forecasting across brands, this isn't your problem and I won't lead with it.
  • Our field service product. We have one. FIELDBOSS is purpose-built for elevator contractors and ours is not. Yours is the better tool for your trade.
  • Marketing Cloud. Nothing about a five-brand elevator roll-up suggests you need campaign automation right now.

Which leaves the data layer, the integration layer, and the action layer — the three that have anything to do with what you actually told me you were building.

The question I asked badly on the call

There's a real difference between asking your data a question and having something act on the answer.

Querying a lake with Claude is genuinely useful and puts you ahead of most of your peers. What it doesn't give you is anything that carries the answer forward — no permissions model, no write-back to the system of record, no audit trail, no owner. That's what I was reaching for and failed to say.

For whoever Chris forwards this to

Five questions I can't answer from the outside — and two of them could rule this out.

I'd rather send questions than a deck. The answers decide whether there's a conversation worth having at all.

What is the lake actually built on?

Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery, Redshift, Microsoft Fabric, or object storage with a catalog over it?

Why it decides things: We can query Snowflake, Databricks, BigQuery and Redshift in place, without copying — Databricks file federation now runs bi-directionally. Microsoft Fabric is not there yet. If you've built on Fabric — a perfectly reasonable choice given Dynamics and Business Central — then the read-in-place story does not apply to you today, and I'd rather say that now than three meetings in.

Is FIELDBOSS data in the lake, or only the financials?

Work orders, safety tests, equipment histories and violations — or just what comes out of Business Central?

Why it decides things: If the operational elevator data never reaches the lake, Claude is reasoning over financial outcomes rather than field reality, and the interesting questions are unanswerable no matter what sits on top.

One lake, or one per brand?

Did Century, Avery, Gen-L, ETI and All City get consolidated into a single lake, or is each still landing separately?

Why it decides things: This is the difference between a reporting project and an integration project. If it's still five, the sequencing matters more than the tooling, and that's a conversation worth having on its own.

How are you calling Claude today?

Direct API, through Bedrock, the desktop app, or something your team built?

Why it decides things: If you're already on Bedrock you're most of the way to running inside a governed boundary and this is a smaller change than it sounds. If it's the desktop app, what you have today is one or two people doing something clever rather than something the business can depend on — still valuable, different starting point.

Who owns identity and permissions for lake access?

Is there a model for who can see which brand's data, or is lake access currently all-or-nothing?

Why it decides things: This is the one that quietly kills these projects. Every governed-agent design assumes an answer, and eighteen months into a five-brand integration there often isn't one yet.

One thing worth knowing, since you're already on Claude

On 26 August, Salesforce and Anthropic announced an expanded partnership. Claude is the first model fully integrated inside the Salesforce trust boundary, delivered through Amazon Bedrock, and it serves as a reasoning model in Agentforce and is available in Agent Builder.

The practical consequence: the work your team is doing with Claude is not something you'd be asked to unwind. Same model. The only question is whether you want a permission and action layer wrapped around it, or would rather build that yourself. Building it yourself is a real option, and I'd want to understand why you'd choose it before arguing against it.
